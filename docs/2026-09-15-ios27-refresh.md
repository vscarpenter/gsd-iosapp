# iOS 27 / macOS 27 refresh: decisions

Date: 2026-09-15. Release: 3.0.0 (34). Branch: `feat/ios27-refresh`.
Brief: the September 15 refresh review and its per-repo kickoff (`design_handoff_ios27_refresh/`).

## The stance

Selective Liquid Glass. System chrome (toolbars, the tab bar, the sidebar, sheet grab regions)
takes the material the OS gives it. Everything Inkwell owns stays opaque: `surfaceCard()`, the
quadrant washes, sheet content, and the matrix. PRODUCT.md carries this as design principle 6
so parity work never glasses the content layer to match iOS.

## Toolchain

- Xcode 27.0 (27A266a), Swift 6.4, iOS 27.0 SDK and simulator runtime.
- `SWIFT_VERSION` in `project.yml` stays "6.0". The brief asks for 6.4, but that setting is
  the language mode, and the compiler accepts only 4, 4.2, 5, and 6. Xcode 27 supplies the
  6.4 compiler on its own. A comment beside the setting says so.
- The deployment floor stays at iOS 26.0 and macOS 26.0. The SDK drives the new look.
- All three targets built with zero errors and zero project warnings on the 27 SDK before any
  source change. The `@State` macro coexists with the property wrapper in SwiftUICore, and the
  app's `State(initialValue:)` sites (`GSDApp.swift`, `SmartViewEditorView.swift`) compile
  untouched.
- No "iPhone 17 Pro" simulator exists on the 27.0 runtime. Builds and captures use iPhone 18
  Pro and iPad Pro 13-inch (M5) on iOS 27.0.

## Scene lifecycle

The app already runs on the scene-based lifecycle: the SwiftUI `App` plus an
`AppDelegate` that answers `configurationForConnecting` with `QuickActionSceneDelegate`.
Quick actions arrive through the window scene (cold launch via `connectionOptions`, warm via
`windowScene(_:performActionFor:)`). The `AppDelegate` copy of `performActionFor` was the
pre-scene path and is dead in a scene-based app, so it is gone. `LiveWebAuthPresenter`
resolves its anchor from `connectedScenes`, and the Share Extension hosts a view controller,
which the requirement does not touch.

## Visual baseline

`scripts/capture-screens.sh <label> [iphone|ipad|mac]` runs
`ScreenshotTests/RefreshBaseline.swift`: the seeded app with a frozen clock, one PNG per
screen, light and dark, on iPhone, iPad, and Mac Catalyst. Output lands in
`build/screens/<label>/`, which is gitignored. The pre-change set is `build/screens/baseline`.
Every later phase compares its touched screens against it.

What the baseline showed before any change:

- The 27 SDK already renders the floating glass toolbar buttons and the floating tab bar with
  no code change. The serif titles survive.
- On iPad the sidebar's selected row now renders as an opaque pill in the app tint (ink), so
  the selected label was ink on ink and unreadable in both appearances. Before the 27 SDK this
  happened only on Catalyst, which the code already handled.

## Phase 1: glass where the system gives it

- `AppAppearance.configure()` no longer builds `UINavigationBarAppearance` or
  `UITabBarAppearance` objects. It sets the serif title attributes on the navigation bar
  proxy and leaves the bar and tab-bar material to the system. On the 27 SDK the change is
  invisible on iPhone (every light screen pixel-identical to the baseline) and the serif
  titles still render, so the simpler code stays.
- The selected sidebar row now draws its label and symbol with `.primary` instead of a
  guessed on-accent color. The selection fill moved in both directions this cycle: opaque
  ink on iPad (the old code kept ink text, unreadable) and a neutral fill on macOS 27 while
  the sidebar is unfocused (the old Catalyst-only paper text painted white on light).
  `.primary` follows whichever fill the system paints.
- The sidebar's Matrix row carries the four-pigment mark (`LaunchMark`) instead of a gray
  grid symbol. Smart-view rows keep their existing identity pigments; Dashboard, Archive,
  and Settings stay graphite. No new colors.
- PRODUCT.md principle 6 records the opacity rule.

### The glass slider extremes

The iOS 27 simulator's Settings app exposes no Display & Brightness page (its lazy root list
never lists the row and its search index answers nothing), so the ultra-clear and fully
tinted extremes could not be captured here. `ScreenshotTests/GlassSetting.swift` drives the
control through Settings for a device run. On paper the chrome holds: the toolbar buttons,
the sync chip, and the sheet controls sit over paper or an opaque sheet, and at the clear
extreme ink on paper is about 14:1 and the teal tint about 6:1, both AA. The tinted extreme
adds material behind the text and only raises those numbers. What a device pass must confirm
is the scrolled state, where cards pass under the toolbar.

## Phase 2: the SDK's own interactions

- iPad matrix cards use the system `.swipeActions` inside a `swipeActionsContainer()` on the
  matrix scroll view, with the same verbs, order, and tints as the iPhone list rows: leading
  Complete (full swipe completes), trailing Snooze then Delete. The system supplies the
  haptics, right-to-left mirroring, and the reveal's VoiceOver actions; the card keeps its
  explicit custom actions, drag-to-move, long-press menu, and tap-to-edit.
- `SwipeRevealRow` is not retired. Both APIs are iOS 27, and the floor stays at 26 as the
  brief asks, so retiring it would drop iPad swipe actions for every iPadOS 26 user. It is
  the `#available` fallback in `QuadrantCell` and goes when the floor moves to 27.
- Toolbar ranking uses `visibilityPriority`, which orders overflow when a toolbar runs out of
  room rather than minimizing on scroll: search stays visible longest, Show Completed and
  Edit overflow first, the sync chip keeps the default. The scroll behavior the brief wants
  comes from `tabBarMinimizeBehavior(.onScrollDown)` on the compact tab bar, so on a
  scrolled matrix the pinned capture bar is the only chrome left in full.

## Phase 3: one root editor

The compact and regular roots swap on `horizontalSizeClass`, and that swap rebuilds every
surface, which is where per-surface sheets die. Tab, sidebar, and Browse-path selection
already lived in the shared `PaletteController`; the task editor now does too
(`PaletteController.editor`), presented once at the root. Five per-surface copies of the
sheet are gone, and the palette's deferred handoff targets the same slot.

### Fixed geometry

- The undo window no longer sits 72 points above the screen bottom. On compact it is the tab
  bar's bottom accessory (`tabViewBottomAccessory`, iOS 26): the system draws the glass,
  keeps it clear of the bar, and folds it beside the bar when the bar minimizes on scroll.
  The regular root keeps the ink capsule overlay. The state moved into a root-owned
  `UndoDeleteController`, which is what lets the root decide where to host it.
- The dashboard's range picker sizes itself (`fixedSize`) instead of a fixed 170 points.
- The capture bar, the editor detents, and the completion rings already scale with the
  window; nothing to change.

### Intermediate widths

`--demo-window <w>x<h>` (Mac Catalyst, screenshot harness only) pins the window to an exact
size, and two Darwin notifications move it between 420 and 1100 points while the app runs.
`RefreshBaseline.testCaptureWidths` captures the matrix at 420, 700, and 1100 points, then
opens the editor at 1100, crosses to 420 (the root swaps to the tab view), and back,
asserting the editor survives both crossings. `scripts/capture-screens.sh <label> widths`
runs it. The Mac is the one target where a size class can cross inside a test.

The set found one defect and two Catalyst artifacts. The defect: the editor chose its sheet
shape by device idiom, so a compact window still got the page-sized sheet and it overflowed.
The presenter's size class now decides (`TaskEditorView(request:compactPresenter:)`), which
also covers a narrow iPad window. The artifacts, both below the app's own 720-point minimum
window and reachable only through the harness hook: a Catalyst sheet keeps its default width
in a 420-point window, and after a synthetic compact-to-regular swap the window toolbar keeps
the tab strip the removed tab view installed. Neither reaches a user.

## Phase 4: icon and widget

- `App/AppIcon.icon` is an Icon Composer package: the paper fill, four opaque pigment tiles
  (their dark variants as `dark` specializations), and the white check as its own layer in
  front, so the 2x2 glyph survives on shape alone in the Clear and Tinted modes. The check
  group is listed first: Icon Composer orders groups front to back, and a check listed after
  the tiles never renders. The tiles keep no translucency; at 0.5 the pigments washed out in
  the flattened preview and the pigments are the brand. The legacy `AppIcon.appiconset`
  stays beside it as the flattened fallback; actool compiles both without complaint.
- Today's Focus rows mark the due state by shape (filled overdue, half due today, outline
  later) with weight on overdue titles. Every row is a Do First task, so there is no quadrant
  to mark. The marker takes the widget tint in full color and `.primary` in the accented and
  vibrant rendering modes, with `widgetAccentable()` so the accent applies.
- There is no menu bar presence to check: GSD has no status item. The widget extension is
  iOS only (`platformFilter: iOS`), so there is no Mac desktop widget either.

## Container reordering: evaluated, not adopted

`reorderContainer(for:move:)` with `.reorderable()` is real and would work on the matrix grid
and the smart-view lists. It is not adopted this cycle:

- In-quadrant order is derived, not stored. `TaskStore.tasks(in:showCompleted:)` sorts by
  `updatedAt` with completed tasks last, and smart views sort by their criteria. Nothing in
  the task model holds a position.
- A manual order needs a persisted field. That field would have to reach the backup envelope
  and the PocketBase wire model for the web and Android clients to agree, which is exactly
  the three-repo contract change the brief rules out.
- Cross-quadrant drag stays as it is: it flips the urgent and important flags, it does not
  move a position.

Revisit only as a cross-platform spec item with a stored order in all three clients.

## Verification

- `cd GSDKit && swift test`: green after every phase (271 Swift Testing tests plus the XCTest
  suites).
- `xcodegen generate` and a build of iPhone, iPad, and Mac Catalyst: zero errors and zero
  project warnings after every phase.
- Screen captures: `baseline`, `phase1`, `phase2`, and `phase3` sets under `build/screens/`,
  light and dark, compared with a pixel diff. Every diff outside the touched region was a
  scroll position or a keyboard strip. The iPad sidebar, the iOS 27 swipe reveal, the
  minimized tab bar, and the dashboard picker are the only intended differences.
- The layered icon renders on the iPad Home Screen in both appearances with the check on the
  rust tile; the widget markers render on the demo Home Screen.
- Mac Catalyst: six screens per appearance in every set, and the width set passes: the
  matrix at 420 (compact tab-bar root), 700, and 1100 points, and the editor opened by ⌘N at
  1100 survives the live resize to 420 and back.

Owner-owed, because the tooling here cannot reach them:

- The Liquid Glass slider extremes on a device, scrolled, on the Matrix, Browse, and
  Dashboard toolbars, the tab bar, and a sheet (`ScreenshotTests/GlassSetting.swift` drives
  the control).
- The icon in the Clear and Tinted Home Screen modes, and the widget in the tinted mode, on a
  device.
- The Mac walk's editor, help, feedback, and capture steps: a card tap and a Settings scroll
  do not register on the Mac in the harness, so those screens are iPad-only captures.
- The swipe actions, drag-to-move, and VoiceOver actions on a physical iPad.
